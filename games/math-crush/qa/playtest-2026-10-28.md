## What works

- Immediate board entry removes the previous briefing friction. The small contextual prompts are sufficient for the observed introductions.
- Stars reward stronger chains without a clock. L2 is now achievable with three straightforward longer chains, and L7 allows a relaxed one-star clear.
- Special blast previews make their effects understandable. Making a stripe and then using it is more satisfying than ordinary pair hunting.
- Rush's move-based duration fits the relaxed game. Keep speed as an optional challenge.
- The phone selection state keeps Clear and Submit accessible. The idle toolbar is near the bottom at 390×844; preserve that space when adding features.

## Findings and proposed next changes

These are proposals for a next design pass, not approved runtime changes. The current implementation largely follows our handover: the repeated targets and short level quotas also originate in that packet.

### 1. Vary the arithmetic rhythm (high priority)

Observed four consecutive levels at 10, followed by four at 12. Source inspection confirms the complete schedule is 10/10/10/10, 12/12/12/12, 15/15/15/15. This encourages repeated pair patterns and makes new levels feel similar.

Start by varying targets BETWEEN levels while keeping each early board's target stable. A candidate sequence for playtesting is **10, 10, 8, 12, 9, 14, 11, 15, 12, 16, 13, 18**. This is an illustrative rhythm, not validated level data. Re-author boards, refill distributions and star thresholds together; changing only the target could remove satisfying long chains. Low targets need enough small values; larger targets are not automatically harder.

Later introduce an occasional “Candy orders” level with a short visible sequence, e.g. **10 → 12 → 9**. Change only after a successful chain, never during selection; show the next order. Validate that the resulting board supports the next target before handing control back. Keep this as a distinctive level type rather than changing every level's target every move.

### 2. Give special moves time and purpose (high priority)

L1–4 finish after three chains; L5–8 after four. Each clear advances the quota equally, whether it is a pair or a spectacular blast. A special earned on the final move has no playable payoff. This limits the space for combinations.

Prototype occasional longer showcase levels and goals such as **clear six frosting patches** or **free two wrapped sweets**. Longer chains and blasts should help complete these goals, giving the hammer a clear purpose too. Keep straightforward chain-count levels between them. Do not just increase every quota or add a timer.

### 3. Fix the Rush payoff window (high priority; source-derived for L9–12)

L8 starts with two charges and worked well: one qualifying chain then all three boosted chains. L9–12 start at zero, require three qualifying chains to activate, and finish after five valid chains. Therefore at most two boosted chains can be used; later activation gives fewer or none. This follows from the level data and rules, not a completed L9–12 playthrough.

For Rush-focused levels, allow enough objective progress after activation to enjoy all three uses, or deliberately redesign the charge/use balance. Preview charge gain while selecting; show a clear “Rush ready” moment and remaining uses. Test this alongside quota/star changes.

### 4. Add feedback around meaningful events (medium priority)

Prioritize a compact “Striped candy!” / “Wrapped candy!” creation reveal at the reward cell, a brief directional sweep for stripes, a soft radial pulse for wrapped blasts, and a larger but short combo celebration. Preserve number legibility and reduced-motion support. These are proposed polish directions; this test did not assess animation timing or audio quality.

On the final move, consider a short automatic celebration from unused specials, with explicitly defined scoring, rather than presenting a newly earned tool the player cannot use. Any extra score would require rebalancing stars. Keep the Next action immediate.

### 5. Make stickers distinct achievements (medium priority)

Perfect Batch arrived with Sweet Start on L1, where no hammer is available. Four of six stickers were earned by L8. The book works, but two immediate awards dilute their meaning.

Restrict Perfect Batch to levels offering a tool, or give it a different explicit criterion. Add a small “next sticker” preview on results/trail so a child has a reason to experiment with combinations. Do not add another pre-play explanation screen or force collection browsing.

## Suggested order

1. Rework target sequence and a small representative set of boards; validate both simple clears and interesting long-chain routes.
2. Prototype one frosting objective and one Rush showcase with a full payoff window.
3. Add creation/combo feedback and revise sticker criteria.
4. Playtest with children before expanding the campaign. This adult agent playtest cannot establish children's comprehension or difficulty.

No runtime code or frozen handover files changed in this review.
