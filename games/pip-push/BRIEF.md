# Pip Push

## The idea
> Pip Push: a Sokoban with dice, where every push rolls the die and each goal wants a specific number on top
> (studio pitch, 22 Oct 2026 — Benji's queue was empty. Runners-up: "Shift Suguru", a solved Suguru scrambled by
> Loopover-style row/column slides that you restore using Suguru rules as the only clue; and "Wall-less Suguru",
> Fillomino-style, where you draw the regions yourself.)

A Sokoban where the crates are dice: every push tips the die over, and each goal square shows the number that must face up when the die arrives.

Why this one: it is a pure logic puzzle (Benji's favourite family), every move is a real decision (where a die
goes *and* which way it arrives), it needs no timer, and it fits one night with a BFS solver behind every level.

## Design
### Core loop
Walk around a small room; walk into a die to push it one square. The die tips over in that direction, so its top
number changes. Get every die onto a goal showing the goal's number. Every few seconds the question is "if I push
from here, what comes up — and do I need a detour to arrive with the right face?"

### The twist
The die's orientation is state. The rule players learn: **the side you push against becomes the top** (push from the
left, the left face comes up). Opposite faces add to 7. Four pushes in a straight line bring the same face back.
Getting the right number usually means a loop or sidestep, and walls limit which loops exist.

### Controls
- Phone: tap a free square to walk there (shortest path, never pushes, one Undo step), tap/swipe next to a die to
  push it once, or use the 4-way pad under the board (hold to repeat). Undo and Restart buttons always visible.
- Desktop: arrow keys / WASD, Z or Backspace = undo, R = restart, N = next level after a win. Mouse clicks work like taps.

### Progression
10 hand-picked rooms from the generators in `lab/` (seeded random rooms; every reachable state explored; goal
numbers chosen so the number matters; `gen2.mjs` also rejects solutions that repeat the same push loop). Order:
1 "First roll" (a corridor, three pushes, can't fail), 2-5 one die (4, 3, 4, 9 pushes; level 4 teaches the
"small loop turns a die" idea), 6 first two-dice level, then two dice up to 20 pushes / 77 steps in level 10. All levels open from the
start (picker shows solved ones); progress saved in localStorage when available.

### Win / lose / restart
Win: all goals satisfied → "Solved" card with moves and pushes vs par, Next. No lose state: stuck dice are fixed with
Undo (unlimited) or Restart (instant, itself undoable).

### Feel
- Die slides and tips with a short roll animation (squash + face swap), 120 ms; reduced motion = instant.
- A goal glows green and pips turn green when a die arrives with the right number; a soft "nope" shake and thud when
  a push is blocked.
- Win chime and a small pip burst; WebAudio blips per step/push, mute toggle.

### Hints and dead ends (night 2)
Hint = the next push of a minimum-push plan from the current position (ring on the stand square, arrow on the die,
"n comes up", pushes left). Plans are cached: the level's stored solution and the last hint's plan answer instantly;
anything else is searched in a Web Worker (page fallback). After each push a capped search (15 000 states) checks
whether the level can still be finished; only a complete search marks it dead, and the tip offers "Undo N moves".

### Ice (night 3)
Map char `~` (ice), `&` (player starting on ice). Push rule: the die moves one square and rolls; while it stands on
ice and the next square is free, it moves and rolls again. The player still steps one square. Drawn as pale blue
tiles with a few diagonal glints; a sliding die animates square by square (about 70 ms per square). Same rule in
`applyMove`, in `pushSolve` (game + `lab/push.mjs`) and in `lab/core.mjs`.
