## MC-022 — shaped boards and jelly objectives
Implement active-cell mask and segmented gravity exactly in RULES.md. Hourglass removes middle-row edge cells10/14, ring removes centre12. Voids aren't obstacles that candies can enter or blast. They break adjacency and vertical gravity segments, but not a stripe's reach across a row. Clear the labelled jelly patches on levels5/9: objective is all jelly removed, no simultaneous chain quota. A valid chain or blast removes one jelly layer per affected terrain cell. Jelly stays fixed beneath falling candies and never respawns. Special rewards don't restore jelly. The level data has single-layer jelly only. Every move now has a spatial purpose; show remaining jelly count.

## MC-023 — changing targets
On sequence levels8/10/12 show current operation/target and next target; advance exactly once after each successful chain. Invalid release, cancelled selection, hints, Undo UI entry and animation frames never advance. The committed model includes the next target before validating/refilling the next playable board, while the UI holds the old target during clear feedback and transitions after settling. No input during that transition. Undo restores target index and board together. Last objective-completing clear wins with no phantom next target/refill. Sequence is authored, not random. On final step show Final target instead of repeating Next. All remaining targets can be read in optional Help, so no hidden rule change.

## MC-024 — map as a journey
Three chapters: Candy Garden(1–4), Chocolate Hills(5–8), Frosting Lake(9–12). Use supplied painted backdrop per region, SVG moving cloud/sparkles, live cream path, real numbered 64px level buttons and supplied board-shape thumbnail. Objective icon + current/best stars give a reason to replay. Current frontier uses raspberry node and you-are-here.svg; don't pulse all nodes. Each node's accessible label includes level/name/objective/state. Locked nodes are muted with lock, never false interactive candy. Tapping an unlocked node starts immediately; no new Play confirmation. Brief text under node names objective/operation, not a modal. Main Continue goes to saved attempt or next unlocked level. Save same campaign and level explicitly.

Chapters form one vertically scrollable map with a sticky chapter label and a bottom safe-area Continue shelf. On entry scroll current node fully above shelf; retain manual scroll on return. At chapter completion animate a ribbon sweep/reveal of next region once (600ms maximum, skippable by tap); never lock navigation behind animation. Stars are displayed only from that campaign/version's records. Existing Classic map may use these chapters with its actual contents; don't advertise jelly/multiplication where absent. Journey preview phaseB shows nodes11–12 as Coming later, no dead tap. All-complete Continue becomes Replay level12 (or10 in phaseB).

## MC-025 — tactile action and calm idle motion
Use MOTION.md's timings and assets. Important order: selection anticipation → chain clear → special travel/burst → fall/settle → score/target update. Distinct selected vs blast-only cells, no digits obscured. Background cloud drift, sparse candy glints and a gentle special idle wobble supply life without moving hit areas. Pause idle loops while selecting, in modals, hidden or reduced motion. Never move every candy simultaneously. Keep idle CPU/GPU work bounded and data independent of animations.

## MC-026 — multiplication pilots (phaseD)
Same 2–8 tile orthogonal gesture, but product instead of sum; a persistent × icon and “Multiply to make N”. Full expression uses × between every selected tile. Levels11/12 only; no mode switch mid-attempt. Example3×4=12;2×3=6 is wrong for12;1×3×4=12 is legal. Values remain1–9, repeated values allowed on distinct cells, no negative/fraction/division. Chain multipliers are score bonuses, shown as “Score ×1.5” so they cannot be mistaken for equation operators. Specials disabled in these two pilots to isolate the arithmetic lesson. Level11 fixed12, level12 sequence18→12→24. No timer. Repair validates product and injects a legal factor pair, not an additive pair. No publication of these pilots before multiplication-specific tests pass.
